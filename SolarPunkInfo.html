Core Beliefs and Goals

Solarpunk places a strong emphasis on coexisting - with nature, technology, and eachother. Things like connection, responisbility, and efficiency are recurring themes throughout the idea. The main goal of solarpunk is to create a world where humanity works with nature to benefit both sides through sustainable energy and creative design.

A Brief History

The idea of Solarpunk all started with the 2008 Republic of the Bees article "From Steampunk to Solarpunk" where the author praises Beluga Project's hybrid ship that uses a skysail in tandem with normal feul to make their transport more efficient. While this article didn't gain much popularity until later, the author's emphasis on blending old and new technology, as well as using clean energy, became prominent points in the solarpunk idea. The idea remained mostly dormant until 2012, when Gerson Loi Rivero published “Solar Punk”, an anthology imagining a society that switched to green energy. It outlines the real effects; both positive and negative.

Then, in 2014, Olivia Luise's tumblr post gave the movement a face. She outlines the aesthetic portion of the movement, allowing it to gain more traction online. Things like stained glass, solar panel windows, and passive architecture branched off of this post. An important part of this post is the mention of education, both in farming and technology. It's easy to fault our modern day technology and want to erradicate it/start fresh. Education about responsible technology use is a key step to avoid restarting. Solarpunk needs a blend of nature and tech to really come alive.

By 2016, the aesthetic became fully established as it spread across platforms like Pinterest. In 2024, it officially became a sci-fi genre with the entrance to the Encyclopedia of Science Fiction. There was another explosion of the movement on platforms like Tik Tok and Instagram in 2026 (aka when this is being written!!). Now, the movement is spreading through groups, small businesses, and even large tech companies. (See "What already exists?")

Architecture

If you have searched for solar punk anywhere on the internet, you've likely seen the stunning vertical forests, glass skyscrapers blooming with trees, and vegetation growing on every rooftop. While these stunning pictures are a great hook, the reality of working and long-lasting Solarpunk architecture needs to be more practical. The idea behind Solarpunk architecture is that it works efficiently and (usually) uses biomimicry, technology, and passive architecture to preserve nature and reduce energy consumption.
